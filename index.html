Rationale

“There is a culture of using abbreviations in the healthcare system and it is widely recognised that the use of abbreviations should be discouraged.”

“However Northern Ireland Hospice recognises it would be challenging to change practice and therefore the risk of using abbreviations should be mitigated to the lowest level. Northern Ireland Hospice has agreed that the glossary of abbreviations attached (appendix 1) may be used.”

Objectives

“To provide an agreed glossary of abbreviations to reduce risk.”

Roles and Responsibilities

“It is the responsibility of all Northern Ireland Hospice employees to adhere to this policy.”

Policy

  • “Documentation and the recording of patient information must be factual, accurate and must not contain jargon.”
  • “Northern Ireland Hospice discourages the use of abbreviations. However when there is already a culture of using abbreviations in the healthcare system and to manage risk, the Northern Ireland Hospice has agreed a glossary of abbreviations attached (appendix 1) may be used.”
  • “Only the abbreviations used in the glossary may be used in the recording of patient care. The Northern Ireland Hospice does not sanction the use of unapproved abbreviations”
  • “Abbreviations must not be used on consent forms except for certain exceptions as indicated in appendix 1.”
  • “The Chief Care Officer and the Medical Leads must agree the use of additional abbreviations and the policy should subsequently be amended and reissued.”
  • “Any further abbreviations relating to individual service groups should be agreed and sent to the Clinical Leads Group.”
  • “Medicine names should not be abbreviated.”
  • “Times must be recorded using the 24-hour clock (AM and PM are not approved abbreviations).”
  • “The word “units” must always be written in full and never abbreviated (for example to U or IU).”

Debbie Burns
Director of Care & Quality Performance, November 2026
